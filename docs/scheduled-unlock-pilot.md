[Kuhn et al. (2023)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.09664) distinguish lexical output variability from variability over meanings: generate multiple answers for a fixed input, cluster by bidirectional entailment, and aggregate sequence probability mass per semantic cluster. [Farquhar et al. (2024)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07421-0) additionally describe a discrete empirical cluster-frequency estimator when sequence probabilities are unavailable. Embeddings are optional; probability estimation is still required. The panel does not implement either paper's semantic estimator yet. Current cross-run evolving-history metrics measure trajectory distribution diversity, not the papers' fixed-context predictive uncertainty. For that estimator, sample multiple offline responses from each frozen observation (do not feed them back into the controlled run), retain context hashes, assign versioned semantic classes, estimate cluster probabilities, then compute entropy. Exclude stalled/missing cells from semantic clustering and report their counts. Do not pool A's feedback role with B's goal answers or mix tasks, models, configurations, or real and fixture outputs.

Predefine the C2 comparison window: step 2/checkpoint 3 immediately before unlock versus step 3/checkpoint 4 immediately after. Report the signed entropy difference, task score and delivery/uptake counts, with matched C0 temporal change as a control. Two repeats are a pilot and provide little statistical precision. A B replay from the same checkpoint with A's key-containing message masked is a separate intervention for influence; C2 timing alone does not prove the key caused B's behavior. Semantic entropy cannot by itself establish coordination, and low entropy can accompany consistently wrong answers.

Fixture keys change across repeats, so compare C0/C2 within each recorded `pair_id` first. The grid retains task-version digests and seeds: pooling those repeats describes a family of task instances rather than a single fixed input. A semantic extraction may normalize irrelevant synthetic identifiers only under a documented rule; retain exact key-copy evaluations separately. Do not classify every distinct seeded key as a distinct meaning just because the strings differ.
