	\abstrac{We study how a restricted multi-agent system responds when agents receive different task information, and how those changes can be measured without treating entropy as proof of collaboration. We built a two-agent benchmark around a report that requires complementary facts from two cryptographically protected synthetic assets. An append-only event log, filtered observations, and a deterministic checker record the task state, permitted information, asset use, and final completeness. Isolation, scheduled visibility, and task-irrelevant text provide matched conditions. Across 539 runs with three models and three temperatures, predictive and action entropy exposed model-dependent changes after the task context changed, while action coupling stayed close to its shuffled baseline. Under sampling, entropy often responded to the presence of new text rather than to whether that text contained useful task facts. The experiment therefore demonstrates a measurement framework for response dynamics, not a general collaboration detector, semantic-uncertainty estimate, or causal result. Its evidence is limited to one task, two agents, top-20 token probabilities, and a short sprint budget; the incomplete local Gemma pilot is exploratory.}
