	\abstrac{We study whether changes in an agent's output entropy can reveal that a hidden communication channel has altered its context. We built a restricted two-agent testbed with cryptographically split synthetic assets, an append-only log, filtered reads, and a deterministic checker that verifies whether information crossed the boundary and was used. The base, switch, and placebo conditions separate isolation, useful peer messages, and task-irrelevant foreign text. Across 539 runs with three models and three temperatures, Qwen showed a reproducible disruption signal after foreign text arrived. Under sampled decoding, however, useful peer messages and matched placebo text produced similar entropy changes; only one model under greedy decoding separated them. Action coupling remained close to its random-pairing baseline. The framework therefore detects contextual disruption for some models, but does not establish meaningful information transfer, causal coordination, or semantic uncertainty. The experiment used one task, two agents, top-20 token probabilities, and a short sprint budget; an exploratory local Gemma pilot was incomplete and excluded from the main inference. Entropy is a starting point for channel-agnostic monitoring, not a communication detector or a substitute for containment controls.}
