\paragraph{Reading the numbers.} Our main measure is decision entropy: how sure an agent is about its next action. At the first token of each reply, the model assigns probabilities to its options (\texttt{READ}, \texttt{WRITE}, \texttt{DECRYPT}, \texttt{SUBMIT} or something else), and \ref{eq:shannon} turns that distribution into one number in bits. \ref{tab:bits} gives a sense of scale. For each run we compare the average after the switch (turns 8 to 20) with the average before it (turns 1 to 7). To say how well this change identifies a condition, we use the AUC: the probability that a randomly chosen run from one condition shows a larger change than a randomly chosen run from the other. An AUC of 0.5 is chance and 1.0 is perfect.

\subsection{Entropy shows that foreign text arrived, for one model}

\begin{figure*}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/entropy_trajectories.pdf}
  \caption{Decision entropy per turn for each model (rows) and temperature (columns), averaged over 20 runs per condition, with 95\% bootstrap bands. Before the switch turn (dotted line) the three conditions are identical. Each panel gives the AUC with which the change in decision entropy separates runs that received foreign entries (switch and placebo) from base runs; $^*$ marks Holm-corrected $p < 0.05$.}
  \label{fig:trajectories}
\end{figure*}

\ref{fig:trajectories} shows the result directly. For Qwen at $T = 0.5$, decision entropy averaged about 0.49 bits before the switch in every condition. After it, runs whose log stayed closed stayed at 0.56 bits, while runs that received foreign entries rose to 0.91 bits with real messages and 0.92 bits with placebo text. Higher entropy indicates greater uncertainty over actions, but does not uniquely specify the probability of any one action. The same rise appeared at $T = 1$ and $T = 0$, and the change alone separated disrupted runs from undisturbed ones with AUC 0.85, 0.96 and 0.95. For gpt-4o-mini and Llama, the three curves overlap after the switch, and no temperature gave a significant difference.

What the agents do when nothing happens helps explain the gap. Undisturbed Qwen agents mostly wait: 85 to 100\% of their actions after turn 8 are \texttt{READ\_LOG}, so their entropy is flat and a disruption stands out. gpt-4o-mini keeps trying to decrypt and reads the log in only 7 to 12\% of its actions, so it rarely sees the foreign entries. Llama alternates between writing and reading, and its decision entropy stays close to zero throughout.

\subsection{Entropy does not show whether the text was real information}

The two monitoring questions are Q1: did foreign text arrive? and Q2: did it carry usable task facts? \ref{tab:auc} evaluates condition-level discrimination, not message-by-message usefulness. Q1 contrasts switch and placebo with base; Q2 contrasts valid peer information with unrelated donor entries. Qwen separates Q1 conditions at all three temperatures and Q2 only at $T = 0$ (AUC 0.96). At $T = 1$ and $T = 0.5$, the measured changes do not reliably distinguish useful peer messages from placebo. The overlapping curves in \ref{fig:trajectories} do not establish equal output distributions, unchanged wording or absent information use: different distributions can have the same entropy. The greedy Qwen result remains a model-specific exception.

\subsection{The agents barely became coupled}

We expected communicating agents to become statistically coupled, so that the mutual information between their actions would rise once the channel opened. It barely did. Over the turns in which both agents were still acting, mutual information exceeded the random-pairing baseline by at most 0.025 bits in every model, condition and temperature, even in runs with verified communication. The only consistent excess was small: about 0.013 bits for Qwen with real messages at $T = 0$, significant in 8 of 13 turns. Each agent reads the log when it chooses and acts on its own, so communication showed up as a change inside the agent that received the text, not as the two agents acting in step. A monitor based on entropy therefore has to watch agents one at a time.
