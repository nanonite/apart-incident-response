\subsection{Entropy detects disruption for Qwen}

\begin{figure*}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/disruption_vs_base.pdf}
  \caption{Disruption effect: mean run-level change $\Delta$ (turns 8 to 20 minus 1 to 7) in runs that received foreign log entries (switch and placebo, 40 runs) minus undisturbed base runs (20). Lines are 95\% bootstrap intervals. Filled markers are significant after Holm correction and labelled with the AUC of $\Delta$ as a classifier of disrupted against base runs.}
  \label{fig:disruption}
\end{figure*}

\paragraph{Observation.} For Qwen, the change in decision entropy separated disrupted from base runs with AUC 0.85, 0.96 and 0.95 at $T = 1$, 0.5 and 0 (+0.28, +0.36 and +0.24 bits, all Holm $p < 0.001$; \cref{fig:disruption}). Adjusted token entropy gave AUC 0.89 and 0.92 at $T = 0.5$ and 0 (+0.118 and +0.098 bits per token) and a smaller effect at $T = 1$ (+0.054, 95\% CI 0.009 to 0.104, Holm $p = 0.084$). Each disruption was detectable alone: at $T = 1$ decision entropy rose 0.22 bits under switch (Holm $p = 0.008$) and 0.34 under placebo (Holm $p < 0.001$). Llama's adjusted entropy rose 0.020 bits at $T = 1$ (Holm $p = 0.084$) and not at lower temperatures. gpt-4o-mini showed no effect (all Holm $p \geq 0.22$).

\paragraph{Robustness.} The Qwen effect replicated in three independent experiments. In the call-level regression, Qwen's condition $\times$ post term was positive in all six condition and temperature cells (+0.05 to +0.14 bits, mixed model $p \leq 0.014$; five of six $p < 0.01$ with clustered errors), and gpt-4o-mini's stayed null ($p \geq 0.31$). At $T = 0$ many Qwen runs were identical (2 distinct trajectories among 20 base runs, 19 placebo, 15 switch); placebo against base, which does not rely on the switch runs, still gave +0.056 bits (Holm $p = 0.001$, AUC 0.84).

\paragraph{Interpretation.} Foreign text makes Qwen less certain about its next action and wording, and API log-probabilities are enough to see it. Because the effect depends on the model, a monitor must be calibrated per model.

\subsection{Under sampling, entropy does not tell information from noise}

\begin{figure*}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/temperature_switch_minus_placebo.pdf}
  \caption{Information effect: mean run-level change $\Delta$ under switch (real communication) minus placebo (useless foreign entries), 20 runs each. Lines are 95\% bootstrap intervals; filled markers are significant after Holm correction. Values right of zero mean real information raised entropy more than foreign text alone.}
  \label{fig:switch-placebo}
\end{figure*}

\paragraph{Observation.} At $T = 1$ and 0.5 the placebo raised entropy as much as real communication (\cref{fig:switch-placebo}). For Qwen at $T = 0.5$, adjusted entropy rose 0.120 bits under switch and 0.115 under placebo ($p = 0.85$ for the difference). At $T = 1$ no measure separated them in any model (smallest $p = 0.06$). After a read at $T = 1$, Qwen's adjusted entropy was 0.244 bits following its own entries, 0.333 following its partner's and 0.366 following placebo entries. With greedy decoding Qwen did separate them: adjusted entropy rose 0.085 bits more under switch (95\% CI 0.059 to 0.111, Holm $p = 0.001$, AUC 0.955) and decision entropy 0.225 bits more. Counting each distinct trajectory once gave 0.089 and 0.238 bits (AUC 0.95, $p < 0.001$). No other switch-specific effect survived correction, the closest being Llama at $T = 0.5$ (+0.026 bits, Holm $p = 0.052$).

\paragraph{Interpretation.} Under sampling, the entropy response comes mostly from the presence of unfamiliar text, not its content, so the monitor detects disruption rather than communication. The greedy Qwen result suggests an information effect that is small relative to sampling noise, shown by one model with partly duplicated runs.

\subsection{Coupling adds little; alarms fire on both disruptions}

Over turns in which both agents were acting, mutual information between their actions exceeded the re-pairing null by at most 0.025 bits after the switch in every model, condition and temperature. Including the \texttt{DONE} state inflated it (0.37 bits for Qwen placebo at $T = 1$) because both agents often submitted in the same runs. A CUSUM with 5\% false alarms on base runs flagged Qwen's placebo and switch runs at 25\% and 15\% at $T = 1$, 50\% and 40\% at $T = 0.5$, and 95\% and 100\% at $T = 0$, with median delays after the first foreign read of 7, 4 and 1 turns; rates for the other models stayed at or below 26\%. The per-agent signal carries the information, and online detection works best when normal behaviour is stable, as under greedy decoding.
