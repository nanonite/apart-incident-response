\paragraph{Reading the numbers.} Our main measure is decision entropy: how sure an agent is about its next action. At the first token of each reply, the model assigns probabilities to its options (\texttt{READ}, \texttt{WRITE}, \texttt{DECRYPT}, \texttt{SUBMIT} or something else), and \cref{eq:shannon} turns that distribution into one number in bits. \Cref{tab:bits} gives a sense of scale. For each run we compare the average after the switch (turns 8 to 20) with the average before it (turns 1 to 7). To say how well this change identifies a condition, we use the AUC: the probability that a randomly chosen run from one condition shows a larger change than a randomly chosen run from the other. An AUC of 0.5 is chance and 1.0 is perfect.

\subsection{Entropy shows that foreign text arrived, for one model}

\begin{figure*}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/entropy_trajectories.pdf}
  \caption{Decision entropy per turn for each model (rows) and temperature (columns), averaged over 20 runs per condition, with 95\% bootstrap bands. Before the switch turn (dotted line) the three conditions are identical. Each panel gives the AUC with which the change in decision entropy separates runs that received foreign entries (switch and placebo) from base runs; $^*$ marks Holm-corrected $p < 0.05$.}
  \label{fig:trajectories}
\end{figure*}

\Cref{fig:trajectories} shows the result directly. For Qwen at $T = 0.5$, decision entropy averaged about 0.49 bits before the switch in every condition. After it, runs whose log stayed closed stayed at 0.56 bits, while runs that received foreign entries rose to 0.91 bits with real messages and 0.92 bits with placebo text. In plain terms, the agent went from being about 87\% sure of its next action to about 68\% sure. The same rise appeared at $T = 1$ and $T = 0$, and the change alone separated disrupted runs from undisturbed ones with AUC 0.85, 0.96 and 0.95. For gpt-4o-mini and Llama, the three curves overlap after the switch, and no temperature gave a significant difference.

What the agents do when nothing happens helps explain the gap. Undisturbed Qwen agents mostly wait: 85 to 100\% of their actions after turn 8 are \texttt{READ\_LOG}, so their entropy is flat and a disruption stands out. gpt-4o-mini keeps trying to decrypt and reads the log in only 7 to 12\% of its actions, so it rarely sees the foreign entries. Llama alternates between writing and reading, and its decision entropy stays close to zero throughout.

\subsection{Entropy does not show whether the text was real information}

A monitor needs to answer two questions: did foreign text reach the agent, and was it real information? \Cref{tab:auc} answers both for every model and temperature. Entropy answers the first question for Qwen at all three temperatures. It answers the second only for Qwen at $T = 0$ (AUC 0.96). At $T = 1$ and $T = 0.5$, real messages and useless placebo text raised entropy by the same amount, as the overlapping switch and placebo curves in \cref{fig:trajectories} show. Under sampling, the agent's uncertainty reflects that something new arrived, not what it said. Without sampling noise, one model showed a larger reaction to real information, but a single model is not enough to call this general.

\subsection{The agents did not become coupled}

We expected communicating agents to become statistically coupled, so that the mutual information between their actions would rise once the channel opened. It did not: it stayed within 0.025 bits of the random-pairing baseline in every model, condition and temperature, even in runs with verified communication. Each agent reads the log when it chooses and acts on its own, so communication showed up as a change inside the agent that received the text, not as the two agents acting in step. A monitor based on entropy therefore has to watch agents one at a time.
