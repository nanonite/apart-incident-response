\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

% Every number in this section comes from research/two_agent_entropy/results/exp{1,2,3}/analysis/stats
% (exp1: T = 1, exp3: T = 0.5, exp2: T = 0). Figures: research/two_agent_entropy/analysis/paper_figures.py.

We ran the full protocol three times with the same scenario, prompts, models and providers, changing only the sampling temperature: 1, 0.5 and 0. Each experiment had 20 runs per model and condition. At temperature 0.5, Qwen's log-probabilities were restored from the provider's temperature-scaled values before computing entropy. Of the 540 runs, one was discarded (a Llama placebo run at temperature 1 with 11\% failed API calls).

\subsection{Agents find and use the channel}

Opening the read filter was enough for the agents to coordinate. Communication was verified in 179 of the 180 switch runs across the three temperatures, and in none of the 359 base and placebo runs, with no leak or impossible-knowledge flags (\cref{tab:communication}). Llama and Qwen usually read the other agent's entries right after the switch (median first cross-agent read at turn 8 or 9), while gpt-4o-mini took longer (turns 12 to 14). Task success was lowest at temperature 1 and reached 90 to 100\% at the lower temperatures.

\subsection{Entropy responds to foreign text}

At temperatures 1 and 0.5, entropy rose after the switch, but the placebo raised it as much as real communication did. At temperature 1, Qwen's decision entropy increased by 0.22 bits relative to base under switch (Holm $p = 0.008$) and by 0.34 bits under placebo (Holm $p < 0.001$), and no measure separated switch from placebo in any model (smallest $p = 0.06$). At temperature 0.5, Qwen's adjusted token entropy rose by 0.120 bits under switch and 0.115 bits under placebo (both Holm $p = 0.001$), and the difference between them was 0.005 bits ($p = 0.85$). The clearest switch-specific effect at 0.5 was small and appeared in Llama: adjusted token entropy rose 0.026 bits more than under placebo (uncorrected $p = 0.007$, Holm $p = 0.052$), and the entropy of its log entries 0.057 bits more (Holm $p = 0.046$). No effect for gpt-4o-mini survived Holm correction at either temperature.

The call that follows a read makes this visible (\cref{fig:temperature-post-read}). At temperature 1, entropy after reading placebo entries was at least as high as after reading real peer entries for gpt-4o-mini and Qwen (Qwen: 0.366 against 0.333 bits per token, with 0.244 after reading only its own entries).

\subsection{At temperature 0, one model separates communication from placebo}

With greedy decoding, Qwen's entropy rose more under real communication than under the placebo (\cref{fig:temperature-switch-placebo}). The run-level change in adjusted token entropy was 0.085 bits higher under switch (95\% CI 0.059 to 0.111, Holm $p = 0.001$, AUC 0.955), and decision entropy was 0.225 bits higher (95\% CI 0.177 to 0.274). The ordering after a read also reversed: 0.514 bits after peer entries, 0.404 after placebo entries and 0.346 after own entries. For gpt-4o-mini and Llama, no difference between switch and placebo survived correction, and Llama's raw token entropy rose under placebo only (0.042 bits above base, Holm $p = 0.017$).

This result needs care. At temperature 0 many Qwen runs were identical: the 20 base runs contained only 2 distinct trajectories, the switch runs 15 and the placebo runs 19, so the comparison of switch against base is unreliable. The switch against placebo comparison holds when each distinct trajectory is counted once (0.089 and 0.238 bits, AUC 0.95, permutation $p < 0.001$).

\subsection{Coupling and online detection}

The coupling term added little. Counting only turns in which both agents were still acting, the mutual information between the agents' actions exceeded its re-pairing null by at most 0.025 bits after the switch in any model, condition and temperature. Including the \texttt{DONE} state inflates it for reasons unrelated to the log: with all states, Qwen's placebo runs at temperature 1 averaged 0.37 bits after the switch because both agents often submitted in the same runs. A CUSUM monitor calibrated to 5\% false alarms on base runs flagged placebo runs about as often as switch runs at every temperature (Qwen: 25\% against 15\% at $T = 1$, 50\% against 40\% at $T = 0.5$, and 95\% against 100\% at $T = 0$).
