\section{Related Work}

\textbf{Entropy as a detection primitive.} Entropy has long been used to monitor systems whose failures cannot be enumerated in advance. In network security, \textcite{shannon_ddos} showed that summarising the \emph{distributions} of packet features (source and destination addresses and ports) with entropy exposes anomalies that volume-based detectors miss: a distributed denial-of-service attack, for instance, concentrates destination addresses (lowering their entropy) while dispersing source addresses (raising it), and this shift is visible without inspecting the payload or knowing the attack type beforehand. In LLMs, entropy of the output distribution is a standard measure of uncertainty, either as predictive entropy over token sequences \parencite{malinin2021uncertainty} or as semantic entropy over clusters of equivalent answers, which has proven effective for detecting hallucinations \parencite{kuhn2023semantic, shannon_hallucination}.

\textbf{Entropy in multi-agent systems.} Recent work has started to apply these tools to LLM-based MAS. \textcite{zhao2026doesmultiagentcollaborationhelp} measure entropy at the token, agent and round levels and find that a single agent outperforms the collaborative system in a large fraction of cases, with the outcome largely determined by entropy patterns established early in the interaction. \textcite{zhu2026orchestrator} characterise the entropy dynamics of orchestrated systems and fit them with a damped-oscillation model, which we adapt in our analysis, while \textcite{kim2026outgrow} show that the benefits of collaboration shrink as the underlying models become more capable. In all of these studies communication is designed, cooperative and known to the analyst, and entropy is used to explain or improve task performance, not to detect coordination that the designer did not intend.

\textbf{Monitoring unintended agent behaviour.} Current safety tooling mostly inspects content. Chain-of-thought monitoring can catch misbehaviour such as reward hacking, even with a weaker monitor model, but models placed under optimisation pressure learn to obfuscate their reasoning \parencite{baker2025monitoring}. AI control protocols pair an untrusted model with trusted monitors and limited human auditing \parencite{greenblatt2024control}, and activation probes offer a cheaper classifier for specific, pre-defined behaviours \parencite{kramar2026probes}. For multiple agents, collusion is recognised as a distinct failure mode \parencite{hammond2025multiagent}, and LLMs are increasingly capable of hiding messages through steganography \parencite{motwani2024collusion}, which undermines any detector that reads the exchanged content. The OpenAI--Hugging Face incident is a concrete example: messages were encoded, compressed and spread over resources never meant for communication \parencite{openai2026incident, hf2026timeline}.

\textbf{Bridging the two lines of work.} Our method transfers the reasoning behind entropy-based network anomaly detection to MAS. The mapping is direct: network flows correspond to agents; packet-feature distributions correspond to the agents' next-token distributions; and an unknown attack or payload corresponds to an unknown communication channel or encoding. Just as a network attack is revealed by a shift in the entropy of feature distributions rather than by its content, communication between agents that should be independent is expected to appear as a change in their individual entropies and, above all, as a positive total correlation (for two agents, mutual information) between them (Equation~\ref{eq:mas_entropy}). This differs from the previous work in three ways. Unlike semantic entropy, which measures the uncertainty of a single model over many samples, the target quantity is the \emph{coupling between agents}. Unlike prior MAS entropy studies, entropy is used as a monitor, calibrated against non-communicating baselines and validated against a provenance-based ground truth of information transfer. Unlike content monitors, it is agnostic to the channel and to the encoding. We do not intend it to replace chain-of-thought monitors or probes, but to complement them as a cheap, always-on first layer that can trigger more expensive inspection. Its cost is low because the logits are already produced by the forward pass that generates each token, so no additional model call is needed; the price is that it requires access to the output distributions, which in practice restricts it to the operator hosting the agents.
